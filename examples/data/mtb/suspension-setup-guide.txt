Suspension setup starts with sag. As a baseline, many riders run about 25-30% sag
on the rear shock and 15-20% sag on the fork for trail riding.

Rebound controls how quickly suspension returns after compression. If rebound is too fast,
the bike feels bouncy and unstable. If rebound is too slow, suspension can pack down
on repeated bumps and lose grip.

Compression damping affects support under braking, pumping, and corner loads.
A small increase in low-speed compression can add mid-stroke support,
but too much can make the bike harsh on roots and reduce small-bump sensitivity.

Tuning should be iterative: change one setting at a time, test on the same segment,
and write short notes after each run.
